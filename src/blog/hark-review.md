**Rating: 10/10**

**Disclosure first:** nobody paid me or asked me to write this. I signed up early because I was curious, and Hark gave me their $100/month plan free for a month. I want that out in the open before I start praising it.

I've tried an embarrassing number of AI tools. Most of them have the same problem: I ask something, get an extremely well-formatted answer, and then spend the next half hour actually doing the thing. Great. Another tab to manage.

Hark was different enough that I gave it access to my actual workflow for a month. Email, calendar, code, notes. I wouldn't do that for a chatbot I didn't trust to stay inside its permissions. Here's what made me keep using it.

## It actually uses a computer

It opens real browsers on its own machines, signs into websites, fills forms and clicks through those horrible dashboards that somehow still exist in 2026. I can hand it something I've been avoiding because it involves three logins and forty tabs, and get back the result instead of instructions telling me how to do it myself. If it needs a code or an approval, it stops and asks.

## It remembers me

The weirdest design choice is that there's no "new chat" button. It's one ongoing conversation. It remembers which project I'm working on, how I like things organised, and whatever I told it last week. I don't have to paste my life story into a new chat every morning. That sounds small until you've spent enough time doing exactly that.

## It lives in my accounts, carefully

I connected my mail, calendar, code and notes. That lets it sort through an inbox, move things around in my week, look over a PR or put research where I'll actually find it again. It asks before spending money, sending anything as me or posting publicly. That's a fairly important detail when you're giving software this much access. In my month using it, it didn't go rogue.

## It works in parallel

This part genuinely caught me off guard. I'll ask it to look into something complicated and it'll open several browsers, check different angles at once, then pull it together. Market research, comparing products, digging through documentation: the tedious tab-hopping part gets done while I'm doing something else. I still read what it gives me. It's research, not an excuse to stop thinking.

## It builds things

I've used it for code, web pages, documents, slides and study material. It even put updating panels on my home screen. One of the stranger things I asked it to do was play through thousands of rounds of software to find what broke. It actually came back with the failures. This is the kind of thing I mean when I say I want an assistant to *work*, not just explain work.

## It's proactive without being annoying

Morning rundowns, reminders, keeping an eye on things I care about. None of this sounds revolutionary in isolation. The difference is that I didn't have to remember to open another productivity app to get any of it.

## The ADHD angle

I have ADHD. Starting tasks, switching between them and remembering all the loose ends is often harder than the work itself. I can get completely absorbed in one thing and forget three others. Having something keep track of that mess has been more useful to me than another model getting a few extra points on a benchmark.

## Anything I'd change?

It isn't magic. Some sites don't cooperate, longer jobs take time, and anything involving your accounts deserves a second look. I would still check the result before relying on it. But when it's blocked, it tells me instead of pretending the task is finished. That's a surprisingly low bar that plenty of AI products still fail.

## Verdict

I'm keeping the 10/10. Not because it's flawless, and definitely not because AI agents are some kind of religion now. It's because I tried it in my own messy workflow for a month and kept finding reasons to use it. Most AI products make impressive demos. This one has been useful on an ordinary Tuesday.

Try it at [hark.com](https://hark.com).
